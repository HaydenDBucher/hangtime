# Hangtime demo script

Before presenting, open the [live product](https://haydendbucher.github.io/hangtime/) in a desktop browser, refresh once, and keep the [pitch deck](https://haydendbucher.github.io/hangtime/pitch-deck.html) in a second tab. Do not describe modeled status as live telemetry.

## 90-second judged demo

### 0:00–0:15 — The job

Say: “It is 9:30 on a Friday and your group is asking the same question in three chats: where is everyone actually going? Hangtime turns the whole night into one decision screen.”

Show the map and ranked destination list without clicking.

### 0:15–0:35 — Read the night

Point to the privacy-preserving crowd clusters, then the ranked list. Open the leading destination.

Say: “We can compare estimated crowd, direction, wait, cover, peak time, and the active deal without identifying who is there.”

Select **Deals**, then **Rides**, to show that the decision includes value and transport—not just popularity.

### 0:35–0:55 — Make a plan

Mark the destination **Heading there**. Open **Ask the crew**, cast a vote, and lock the winner.

Say: “The utility converts browsing into a group decision in a few actions. That is the behavior our pilot will measure.”

### 0:55–1:12 — Optional social layer

Scroll to crew matching, open one crew profile, and show member photos, plans, mutuals, and verification signals.

Say: “Matching still exists, but only after the place and plan are relevant. Direct handles stay hidden until interest is mutual.”

### 1:12–1:30 — Evidence and candor

Say: “Test conversations moved us from matching-first to map-first. Today the flow is real; the crowd, deal, wait, and ride data are modeled. The next two-week pilot tests decision time, return, data accuracy, and whether three of ten venues will pay.”

## Five-minute demo and pitch

### Minute 0–1 — Customer and pain

- Customer: college crews deciding what to do tonight.
- Pain: current information is spread across group chats, social feeds, venue pages, maps, and ride apps.
- Payer: venue GM, owner, or marketer buying measurable demand and redemption.

### Minute 1–2:30 — Working path

1. Start on the map.
2. Explain aggregate clusters and privacy.
3. Compare the top three ranked destinations.
4. Filter Deals, Events, Food, and Rides.
5. Open one destination and explain the decision card.
6. Mark Heading there.
7. Ask the crew, vote, and lock the plan.
8. Briefly show optional matching and account creation.

### Minute 2:30–3:20 — What changed

Show slide 4 in the deck. Explain that early Hangtime centered on matching groups. Testers wanted a view of where people were going and more information about the entire night. The map, rankings, deals, rides, and privacy model are direct responses—not decorative features.

### Minute 3:20–4:10 — Test and economics

State the four pass/fail metrics exactly. Explain the $149/month venue pilot and the base scenario, then say clearly that the pricing and unit economics are hypotheses.

### Minute 4:10–5:00 — Candor and next move

Name the modeled data, local prototype accounts, and missing production controls. Close with the two-week OSU pilot and ask for one of three concrete things: pilot students, five participating venues, or permission to recruit on campus.

## Recovery if connectivity fails

- Keep screenshots or a screen recording of the full path locally.
- Use the browser deck; it is deployed as a static file with no runtime API dependency.
- Explain that the map tiles require network access but the surrounding prototype and modeled data build locally.
- Never improvise a claim that a failed external feed was “live.”

## Presenter checklist

- Replace every bracketed evidence field in the research materials.
- Practice the 90-second route until it consistently fits.
- Use a fresh browser session and test every button.
- Keep browser zoom at 100% and disable notification banners.
- End on the test, not on a generic vision statement.
