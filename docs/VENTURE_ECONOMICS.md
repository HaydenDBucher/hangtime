# Venture economics

All figures below are hypotheses for a campus pilot, not revenue or traction already achieved.

## Business model

Students use Hangtime free. Local venues, event promoters, and late-night food partners pay to reach groups at the moment they are choosing a destination.

The initial venue offer combines:

- Verified partner listing and basic tools: $149/month.
- Promoted placement budget: $150/month average in the base case.
- Verified offer redemption: $1.25 each.
- Aggregate outcome report: views, saves, heading-there intent, and redemptions.

Hangtime does not need to sell individual location or identity data. The product should report aggregate intent and outcomes.

## Base campus model

| Input | Base assumption |
|---|---:|
| Addressable venue/food/event partners | 30 |
| Paying partners | 15 |
| Partner penetration | 50% |
| Subscription per partner/month | $149 |
| Promotion spend per partner/month | $150 |
| Redemptions per partner/month | 80 |
| Fee per redemption | $1.25 |
| Direct service cost per partner/month | $70 |
| Campus operating cost/month | $1,250 |
| Venue CAC | $450 |

Revenue per paying partner is $399/month: $149 subscription + $150 promotion + $100 redemption fees. Campus revenue is $5,985/month. Direct venue service costs total $1,050 and campus operations total $1,250, leaving $3,685 monthly contribution, or 61.6%.

Contribution before campus overhead is $329 per venue, so a modeled $450 venue CAC pays back in about 1.4 months. A safer operating target is under four months because churn and support cost have not been measured.

## Scenario sensitivity

| Scenario | Paying partners | Revenue/partner | Campus revenue | Campus contribution | Contribution margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | 8 | $214 | $1,712 | -$128 | -7.5% |
| Base | 15 | $399 | $5,985 | $3,685 | 61.6% |
| Bull | 22 | $659 | $14,498 | $10,718 | 73.9% |

The bear case is important: with eight partners, weak promotion demand, and only 40 redemptions per venue, the campus loses money. Partner density and provable conversion are the central economic risks.

## Student acquisition

The working hypothesis is a blended activated-student acquisition cost at or below $2 through campus ambassadors, venue QR codes, event partnerships, and group invites. This is not included as a proven metric. The pilot should measure cost per activated user, not downloads.

An activated student compares at least two destinations and saves, shares, votes on, or marks a plan. Virality should be measured as additional activated participants per shared crew plan.

## What must be validated

1. Interview 10 venue decision-makers and request a signed $149/month pilot or LOI.
2. Measure actual redemption rate and incremental visits, not impressions.
3. Determine venue churn risk after the first month.
4. Measure staff time and verification cost per partner.
5. Test whether promoted placement harms student trust.
6. Replace modeled CAC with actual founder time, travel, materials, and promotion spending.

## Expansion logic

Only expand after one campus demonstrates recurring student use, credible data, and positive contribution. The repeatable unit is a dense campus nightlife corridor, not an entire city. Expansion before local density would weaken both the student product and the payer proposition.
