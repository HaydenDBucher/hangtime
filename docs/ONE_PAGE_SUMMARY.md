# Hangtime

## Know where to go before you go

Hangtime is a campus night-out decision layer. It combines aggregate crowd direction, destination rankings, deals, events, food, waits, cover, ride estimates, and private crew coordination so students can choose a worthwhile plan quickly.

### The problem

College crews currently assemble the night from group chats, social feeds, venue pages, event listings, maps, and ride apps. The information is fragmented and time-sensitive. Groups lose time, miss offers, absorb surge costs, and still risk choosing a dead or overcrowded destination.

### Customer and payer

- **User:** 18–24-year-old college students making a same-night group decision near campus.
- **Economic buyer:** venue owners, GMs, and marketing managers seeking measurable student traffic.
- **Model:** free student utility; paid partner tools, promoted offers, verified redemptions, and aggregate outcome reporting.

### What changed

The original concept led with matching groups. Exploratory testers instead emphasized knowing where people were and understanding the entire night. Hangtime became map-first: destination intelligence and planning are primary, while matching remains an optional, privacy-controlled layer.

### Working MVP

The public prototype supports map and ranking discovery, filters, destination detail, crew intent, voting, plan locking, group profiles, mutual matching, and account flows. Crowd, wait, deal, and ride information is currently modeled; production telemetry and safety operations are not yet built.

### The next honest test

A two-week OSU pilot will test four pre-registered thresholds:

- At least 40% of crews choose and share/lock a destination in two minutes.
- At least 25% of activated users return within 14 days.
- At least 70% of sampled statuses are accurate within 20 minutes.
- At least 3 of 10 venue decision-makers sign a $149/month pilot or LOI.

### Ask

We are seeking 30–50 student testers, five participating campus-area venues, and 10 qualified venue buyer conversations.
