# Neutral research guide

## Student discovery interview — 15 minutes

Do not show the prototype during the first half.

1. Tell me about the last time you and friends decided to go out near campus.
2. When did the conversation begin, and who made the final call?
3. Walk me through every app, page, and message you checked.
4. Where did the group disagree or stall?
5. What information did you wish you had but could not get?
6. Did the group change destinations? Why?
7. What did uncertainty cost you—time, money, missed plans, or something else?
8. How often does a situation like that happen?
9. What information about other people would feel useful? What would feel invasive?
10. Have you tried to solve this another way? What happened?

Avoid “Would you use an app that…?” and “Do you like this idea?” They invite hypothetical approval.

## Prototype task — 10 minutes

Read this prompt exactly:

> It is Friday at 9:30 p.m. Your group wants to go out near campus but has not picked a place. Use this prototype to decide where you would go and send or lock the plan. Think aloud. I will not explain the interface unless you are fully blocked.

Before the task, ask: “From 1 to 5, how confident are you that your group can choose a good destination quickly?”

Observe without leading:

- First click and first data point noticed.
- Whether the user understands crowd clusters and rankings.
- Whether they compare at least two destinations.
- Time to a decision.
- Whether they find deals, rides, and planning.
- Whether they hesitate around location or identity.
- Whether they complete share, vote, lock, or heading-there behavior.

After the task, ask:

1. What made you choose that destination?
2. What did you distrust or ignore?
3. What did you expect to happen that did not?
4. What information was unnecessary?
5. What would prevent you from opening this on your next night out?
6. Again, from 1 to 5, how confident are you in the decision?
7. May we contact you for one follow-up on your next eligible night?

## Venue interview — 20 minutes

1. How do you currently bring in students on a typical night?
2. What do you spend monthly across promotions, creators, ads, and discounts?
3. How do you know which promotion caused a visit?
4. When are you under capacity, and what is an incremental group worth then?
5. Which offers move traffic without destroying margin?
6. Who controls this budget and what approval is required?
7. What result would make a campus demand tool worth paying for?
8. What data would you refuse to share or receive?

Then show only the venue value proposition and outcome report. Ask for a paid pilot with a specific price and start date. Record the response verbatim.

## Session record

For every session, assign a participant ID and enter the date, segment, method, observation, quote, artifact link, decision, and confidence in `EVIDENCE_LOG.csv`. Obtain consent before recording. Remove names and sensitive location details from shared notes.
