H The customer
01 / Customer · pain · payer

A recurring decision with bad information.

User

College crews, 18–24

Choosing where to go tonight near campus.

Pain

Six sources. No answer.

Chats, social feeds, venue pages, events, maps, and ride apps all show only part of the night.

Payer

Venues and promoters

Managers pay for measurable nearby demand—not another vague impression.

The job: choose a worthwhile destination quickly.Free student utility · paid venue demand tools

H Core hypothesis
06 / Honest test

If crews can see the night in one place, they will decide faster and return.

Behavior, not compliments

Give crews a destination task. Do not explain the interface. Measure time, actions, decision, and share/lock completion.

Original vs. revised

Compare the groups-first path with the map-first ecosystem path under the same task.

H Pass or fail
07 / Pre-registered thresholds

We decide with numbers.

Crews choose + share or lock in ≤2 minutes≥40%
Activated users return within 14 days≥25%
Status samples accurate within 20 minutes≥70%
Venue managers sign paid pilot or LOI≥3 / 10
Kill criterion: fewer than 2 of 10 qualified venue decision-makers commit at $149/month means the payer or offer must materially change.
H Evidence status
08 / Candor

Built enough to test. Not yet proven.

Real today

  • Deployed responsive interface
  • Map, ranking, filters, planning, voting
  • Profiles, privacy states, account adapter
  • Optional event API integration

Modeled or unproven

  • Crowds, waits, most deals and events
  • Ride prices are estimates
  • No production moderation or telemetry
  • No measured retention or signed payers
The prototype demonstrates the experience, not traction.Evidence gaps are pilot inputs.
