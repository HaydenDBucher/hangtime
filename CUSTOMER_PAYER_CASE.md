# Customer, Pain, and Payer Case

## Specific customer

The initial customer is an OSU-area student coordinating a group of three or more people who intend to go out tonight but have not agreed on a first destination.

## Costly job and pain

The coordinator must reconcile different preferences, timing, prices, crowd levels, travel effort, and safety expectations while the information is changing. The cost is delay, decision fatigue, group-chat churn, fragmented attention, unnecessary rides or covers, and occasionally an abandoned night.

Quantities such as minutes lost, apps checked, and money wasted are hypotheses until recorded from real customers.

## Buying and usage moment

The user need is urgent and perishable: it appears in the hours before or during a night out. Information loses value quickly, so Hangtime organizes the decision around tonight, the current group, and one locked first destination.

## Current alternatives

- Repeated group-chat polls and messages
- Instagram and venue social feeds
- Google or Apple Maps
- Event-listing services
- Calling, messaging, or individually checking venues
- Letting the most vocal person decide

The alternative is not "nothing"; it is a fragmented manual workflow.

## Value proposition

Hangtime combines group preferences, destination comparisons, modeled crowd and cost signals, and one explicit crew vote so the group can commit to a first stop with less coordination overhead.

## Proposed payer

The proposed payer is a campus-area venue owner, manager, or promoter responsible for demand generation. The venue is economically motivated when it needs to fill a time window, attract groups rather than isolated visitors, or measure whether an offer caused a commitment.

## Why the payer might buy

Hangtime sells a measurable group action rather than an impression: a group selects a venue, commits, and can redeem a time-limited offer. The proposed base offer is $75 per month plus $1 per redeemed group offer, with flat and usage-only alternatives available in the payer test.

## Evidence boundary

This document makes the logic explicit. It does not claim that frequency, pain magnitude, conversion, or willingness to pay has been externally validated.
